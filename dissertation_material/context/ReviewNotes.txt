Abstract
This dissertation investigates whether an AI-driven system can increase produc-
tivity and consistency in year-end accounting note preparation while maintaining
professional quality, auditability, and ethical compliance. The project explores an
end-to-end workflow that integrates Xero accounting data with an AI-assisted note
generation system. The approach combines deterministic numeric reconciliation,
provenance tracking, and constrained natural language generation to automate the
drafting of key financial statement notes. The research evaluates productivity gains,
factual accuracy, and professional acceptability through a human-in-the-loop review
with accountants, while addressing anonymisation, ethical boundaries, and general
research risk assessment.
1. Introduction
Research question:To what extent can an AI-driven system improve productivity and
consistency in the preparation of year-end accounting notes while maintaining professional
quality, auditability, and ethical compliance?
Short motivation:Preparing year-end notes is time-consuming and judgement-
heavy. An automated system that reliably produces draft notes with clear provenance
could materially increase efficiency for accounting firms, reduce routine work for ac-
countants, and improve consistency across clients—while preserving human oversight for
judgemental items.
2. Background / Literature Review (summary)
Why the problem is significant:
•Year-end notes (fixed assets, accruals, loans, related parties, contingencies, etc.)
are mandatory and require reconciliation of ledger balances, explanations of move-
ments, and disclosure of judgemental events. Producing these notes manually
consumes many billable hours.

3. Aim and Objectives
Aim:Design, implement and evaluate a prototype system that automatically generates
year-end accounting notes from Xero data, providing auditable numeric reconciliations,
explanatory narrative, and provenance sufficient for an accountant to review and sign-off.
Objectives:
1. Select and define 3–5 target note types to automate (e.g., Fixed Assets; Trade
Receivables; Loans & Borrowings; Revenue Recognition; Prepayments/Accruals).
2. Build a reproducible ETL pipeline to extract, transform and store relevant Xero
data.
3. Define accounting mapping tables and deterministic rules to compute the numeric
backbone for each note.
4. Develop a provenance model linking every computed figure to the source transac-
tions/journal entries.

4. Methodology
Approach:Mixed-methods engineering research combining systems engineering (ETL,
rules), AI/NLP (LLM / RAG), and human evaluation.
Methods:
•Data acquisition & pre-processing:Use Xero sandbox or anonymised firm
data. Implement OAuth2 extraction and normalize for deterministic queries.
•Rules & analytics layer:Encode accounting rules in Python/dbt. Compute
balances, movements, and adjustments with full traceability metadata.
•LLM integration:Use a high-quality model (API or hosted) with constrained
prompts to ensure numeric and factual consistency.
•Hallucination mitigation & explainability:Only permit the model to state
numeric facts present in structured input; require provenance for each numeric
statement.
•Human-in-the-loop review:Accountants review AI-generated drafts, marking
edits and feedback to evaluate trust and productivity.
•Evaluation methodology:Measure factual accuracy, completeness, edit dis-
tance, time savings, and qualitative trust ratings.
5. Implementation
High-level architecture:
1. Data Layer: Xero API→ETL→PostgreSQL / Parquet.
2. Rules Engine: Python/dbt transformations for note computations.
3. Provenance Store: Mapping from computed figures to source transactions.
4. LLM Service: Receives JSON and returns draft notes with provenance.

•Completeness of required disclosures.

7. Research Risk Assessment
•Data privacy:Use anonymised/sandbox data; encrypt local storage.
•Model hallucination:Use deterministic numeric rules and provenance-enforced
generation.
•Ethical non-compliance:Restrict to draft generation; human approval required.
•Bias or misrepresentation:Human review and transparency logs.
•Technical risk:Use SDKs and fallback models.
A general institutional Research Risk Assessment form (low-risk category) will ac-
company the dissertation submission.
8. Professional, Social, Economic and Legal Issues
Ethical considerations, anonymisation, and boundaries
All datasets are anonymised or sourced from Xero sandbox. The system’s boundary ends
atdraft note generation— it does not perform audits or final sign-off. Human accoun-
tants retain full professional responsibility. Data handling complies with UK GDPR and
institutional ethics guidelines.
Professional, social, and economic implications
•Productivity improvements free accountants for higher-value work.
•Role shifts toward judgement and advisory tasks.
•Ethical deployment requires transparency and accountability.
Legal and compliance
•Adherence to IFRS/UK GAAP disclosure standards.
•GDPR-compliant anonymisation and consent.
•Final liability lies with the professional reviewer.
